From SEO to GEO: how to get your site cited by AIs

Ranking at the top of Google still matters, but it's no longer the only goal. When the question is asked to an AI, the answer comes ready-made — and what counts is your site being the source it cites.

UNDERNEATHOctober 25, 20263 min read

What changed in the way people search

According to Pew Research Center's 2026 survey, about half of U.S. adults now use AI chatbots like ChatGPT, Gemini or Copilot — up from a third in 2024. A growing share of everyday questions that used to start with a Google search now start with a conversation on ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity or in AI Overviews, the AI-generated answers Google itself shows at the top of the page.

The difference is huge: in a list of results, the user chooses among 10 links. In a generated answer, they receive a ready-made text that cites few sources — sometimes none. Whoever isn't cited simply doesn't exist in that answer.

SEO and GEO: what each one is

SEO (Search Engine Optimization) is the work of getting a site to rank well in search results. GEO (Generative Engine Optimization) is the work of getting a site used as a source and recommended in AI-generated answers.

One doesn't replace the other. AIs consult the web to build answers and favor sources that are already easy to find, understand and trust. In practice, GEO is built on top of a well-done SEO foundation — just with higher demands for clarity and authority.

What makes an AI cite a website

  • Clarity about who the business is: what it does, for whom, where it operates. If the site itself is vague, the AI has nothing to repeat.
  • Direct answers: pages that answer a specific question right at the start, without beating around the bush, are easy to use in a generated answer.
  • Structured data: markup describing the organization, services, products and FAQ in a format machines can read without guessing.
  • Consistency beyond the site: the same name, address, phone number and description on the website, the Google Business Profile and social media.
  • Authority signals: reviews, mentions on other sites, cases and proprietary data that confirm the business is real and relevant.
  • Technical access: fast, indexable pages with no unnecessary blocks for crawlers.
In practice

Ask ChatGPT and Gemini what your business does and who the reference for your service is in your city. The answers show how AIs see your business today — and what's missing for you to be included.

How to start working on GEO

  • Rewrite your service pages to answer, in the very first paragraph, what you do and for whom.
  • Create a FAQ page with short, complete answers.
  • Implement structured data for your organization, services and FAQ.
  • Standardize your business information across every channel.
  • Publish what only you have: numbers, cases, processes and the opinions of the people who deliver the service.

None of these actions is a trick to "fool" the AI. They are the same foundations that make a site get found on Google — applied with more rigor.

In the 4 UNDERNEATH layers

GEO depends on all 4 layers at once: findability so the AI can access the site, relevance so the page answers the question, trust so it gets chosen as a source and action so people arriving through the recommendation become contacts.
